Position, Velocity, Acceleration

№ 1 · physics · kinematics

Motion is described by three quantities that stack: position tells you where a body is, velocity tells you how that position changes with time, and acceleration tells you how the velocity changes. Each is the derivative of the one before it.

In one dimension, if $x(t)$ is position then $v(t) = dx/dt$ and $a(t) = dv/dt = d^2x/dt^2$. Constant acceleration collapses this to the familiar $x(t) = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2$.
