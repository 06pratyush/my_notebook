Performance Metrics

№ 5 · machine learning · evaluation

How do you judge a model's worth? Not by how well it fits the training data — any model can memorise — but by how honestly it captures the underlying structure.

R² (Coefficient of Determination)

$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum(y - \hat{y})^2}{\sum(y - \bar{y})^2}$
Lies between 0 and 1. Closer to 1 means less error, better prediction. Also called goodness of fit.

Adjusted R²

$R^2_{adj} = 1 - \frac{(1 - R^2)(n - 1)}{n - k - 1}$
Penalises the model for adding useless variables. k = number of input dimensions.

Mean Squared Error (MSE)

$MSE = \frac{\sum(y - \hat{y})^2}{N}$
Squaring punishes large errors more than small ones.

Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)

$RMSE = \sqrt{MSE}$
Brings the error back into the original units of y.

Mean Absolute Error (MAE)

$MAE = \frac{\sum|y - \hat{y}|}{N}$
More robust to outliers than MSE.

Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r)

$r = \frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2 \cdot \sum(y_i - \bar{y})^2}}$
Measures how two variables are correlated. Ranges from −1 to +1.
